## The Multi-Million-Dollar Mirage and the 4% Illusion

You have probably heard the version of FIRE that circulates most widely online:

> Save up *X* million dollars, put it all into SPY (the S&P 500 index), and strictly follow the 4% safe withdrawal rule. With SPY's historical 7%–10% annualized return, the real growth after inflation covers your living expenses while the real purchasing power of your principal remains intact forever—granting you lifelong financial independence. (*X* simply depends on your target lifestyle.)

The seduction of this narrative lies in its mathematical neatness and the clear, attainable hope it offers. Warren Buffett famously remarked that "nobody wants to get rich slow," and that buying a low-cost S&P 500 index fund is the best way for an ordinary person to build wealth. It makes every present sacrifice and act of frugality feel like another brick laid in the fortress of your future retirement—nothing wasted, delayed gratification guaranteed to compound into a higher form of freedom.

To be fair, this is not what the 4% rule actually claims. The original research—Bengen (1994) and the Trinity Study (1998)—asked a narrower question: can an inflation-adjusted withdrawal last 30 years without running out of money? It never promised a perpetual principal, let alone a fixed place in society. FIRE itself isn't wrong. What this essay pushes back on is the line the popular version quietly adds: *and then you're set for life.*

Yet once we bring credit expansion and the inflation of scarce resources into the frame, the popular version of FIRE leads somewhere else. I propose a name for that condition: **FADE (*Financially Aground, Depreciating Early*)**—the shadow side of FIRE's *Retire Early*.

FIRE does deliver what it promises: a CPI-measured lifestyle that will very likely last. But those who take that as proof they've made it for good have quietly run aground in the shallows of purchasing power. As society's wealth pool keeps expanding, they stand still, and their ability to command scarce resources wears away year after year.

## What Wealth Actually Means

Li Lu—founder of Himalaya Capital and Charlie Munger's longtime partner—has returned to the same definition in several talks: **wealth is your share of the economy's total purchasing power.**

Put simply, **wealth is not an absolute number; it is a share.**

The fatal flaw of the classic 4% FIRE model is that it treats wealth as a static, absolute figure (*X* million dollars). In a stationary society, the two are identical—having more millions naturally means holding a larger share. But the moment we introduce time, society advances and the aggregate pool of purchasing power expands. Even if your *X* million dollars never loses a cent—even if it doubles or grows tenfold in nominal terms—so long as your capital grows more slowly than the total wealth pool of society, your slice of the pie is shrinking, and your real wealth is depreciating.

Many assume that once their portfolio can reliably spin off $200,000 a year, they have "made it." Seen through the lens of relative share, however, they have entered a textbook state of **FADE (*Financially Aground, Depreciating Early*)**:

- **Financially Aground**: Your nominal net worth may never hit zero, but because it fails to keep pace with the roughly 7% annual expansion of the macro asset pool, your claim on scarce social resources runs aground in the shallows, increasingly cut off from the deep-water resources at the top.
- **Depreciating Early**: Driven by the relentless inflation of scarce inputs like premier healthcare and elite education—a phenomenon known as Baumol's cost disease—your slice of purchasing power is eroding far faster than official inflation figures suggest.

What, then, is the true meaning of investing in SPY?

SPY is a remarkably faithful proxy for the expansion of society's productive assets. Many people mistake buying an index fund for riding an elevator to the penthouse; in reality, **going all-in on SPY simply buys you a standing spot on an endlessly rising escalator. It is an exceptional *wealth-anchoring instrument* that bolts your financial scorecard to your current rung on the social ladder.**

But notice the cardinal precondition: you must leave every dividend and every ounce of compounding inside the pool. Withdrawing 4% a year is the equivalent of taking half a step backward down that escalator every twelve months.

## The Purchasing-Power Pyramid and Baumol's Cost Disease

At this point, anyone familiar with the Trinity Study will object: "The 4% rule is CPI-adjusted! If my withdrawals already keep pace with inflation, why wouldn't I be able to afford my current lifestyle?"

Here lies the most insidious blind spot in the entire 4% illusion: **you are hedged against the CPI (Consumer Price Index), but you are completely exposed to *Baumol's Cost Disease*. Simply put, manufactured goods are abundant, but human service and social rank are permanently scarce.**

In a modern economy, prices suffer from a profound split personality:

1. **Scalable manufactured goods and baseline compute (smartphones, appliances, prepared food, streaming)**: Driven by technological progress, their marginal cost trends toward zero, keeping them in secular **deflation**. This is why headline CPI looks so benign.
2. **Non-replicable human time and positional goods (a top surgeon's operating slot, full-time private care, senior legal counsel, prime school-district real estate)**: None of these can be mass-produced on an assembly line. Their prices are never anchored to CPI; they are anchored directly to **the expansion rate of society's total wealth pool**—because you are bidding for the exact same person's finite hours against the richest participants holding newly minted capital gains.

Over the long run, SPY's return and the expansion of society's wealth pool run at roughly the same pace—around 7% a year—which is exactly why it works as an anchor. Withdraw 4% a year, though, and your portfolio falls about four points behind the pool. Year after year, that 4% gap quietly erodes your ability to command other people's time.

Money, after all, is never just a number on a ledger; it is **a claim on other people's time and skill**.

Take total U.S. household net worth as the benchmark. According to the Federal Reserve's [Z.1 Financial Accounts](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/), it stood at about $196 trillion in Q2 2026; for easy mental math, I'll round it to $200 trillion. If you hold $1 million in net worth, you own roughly 5 parts per million (**5 PPM**) of the national wealth pool.

Where does that ratio sit on the real-world spectrum of resource mobilization?

> **Benchmark**: U.S. household net worth ≈ $200 trillion (≈ $196T as of Q2 2026) ｜ 1 PPM ≈ $200,000

| Tier | Net Worth & Share (U.S. Benchmark) | Capacity to Mobilize Social Resources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **T1 · Survival Input Tier** | < $200K<br>(< 1 PPM) | **System inputs.** Unable to mobilize anyone else's specialized intellect. Life hours are locked into baseline labor, leaving one acutely vulnerable to external shocks like illness or job loss. |
| **T2 · Decent Salaried Tier** | $500K – $1M<br>(2.5 – 5 PPM) | **Seasoned cogs.** Full access to scalable industrial abundance (electronics, cars, everyday dining). Yet non-replicable high-tier services (private education, core real estate, top-flight legal defense) still threaten to wipe out life savings. |
| **T3 · Time-Sovereign Tier (FIRE)** | $3M – $5M<br>(15 – 25 PPM) | **Offline observers of the system.** Personal time sovereignty bought at the price of surrendering systemic leverage. Immune to everyday price tags, yet unable to retain dedicated specialist teams or outbid higher tiers during major medical crises or scarce-resource auctions. |
| **T4 · Resource-Allocating Tier** | $13M – $25M<br>(65 – 125 PPM) | **Employers of specialized intellect.** Routinely retain bespoke white-collar teams (private counsel, wealth advisors, concierge medical teams) to execute their will, achieving full immunity against labor inflation. |
| **T5 · System-Architect Tier** | ≥ $100M<br>(≥ 500 PPM) | **Chief architects of social productivity.** No longer merely consuming society, but compiling it. A single capital allocation can mobilize thousands of top engineers and scientists worldwide to attack a technological frontier and shape industrial trajectories for decades. |

Once we unfurl this pyramid, the true nature of the two strategies becomes unmistakable:

- **The reality of continuously dollar-cost averaging active cash flow into SPY**: Like rock climbing, SPY is the piton you hammer into the cliff face to lock in your current elevation so you never slip backward; your ongoing active cash flow is the muscle propelling you toward the next hold.
- **The reality of pulling the trigger on 4% FIRE too early**: You voluntarily forfeit your chips to climb toward the System-Architect tier, unclip your upward rope, and choose a comfortable, controlled, yet unmistakable descent down the mountain.

Many believe that parking $5 million in a broad index fund and drawing $200,000 a year while preserving nominal principal achieves "perpetual wealth." Through the lens of purchasing-power share, **it is in fact a controlled-shrinkage strategy that trades *strategic agency* for *middle-class comfort***. We are entirely free to walk down the mountain and enjoy the scenery—entering a state of graceful **FADE**—so long as we never delude ourselves into thinking we are standing on the summit.

## The Nature of Comparison and the Freedom of Staying in the Arena

Following this logic to its root has also given me a deeper compassion for a very human impulse: keeping up with the Joneses.

I used to think that our obsession with comparing wealth was pure vanity or petty envy—an inability to stand seeing others succeed. Now I see it differently: **status anxiety over slipping behind is, at bottom, an evolutionary survival instinct wired into our DNA—because wealth is inherently relative, serving as your bidding paddle in the auction for scarce resources.** When others' percentage share rises, your priority in the queue for healthcare, education, and physical security is passively pushed back.

Paradoxically, seeing the split between *industrial abundance* and *positional bidding* is precisely what sets us free:

On one hand, we stop being lulled by nominal numbers. True financial freedom may never have been about severing ties with society as early as possible and tiptoeing through life on a 4% allowance. Rather, it is about **using broad-market indexing (Beta) to forge an unbreakable safety net above the survival floor—and using that security as the launchpad to pursue work that generates active cash flow (Alpha) with joy and without short-term desperation.** As long as you are still creating value and feeding even a trickle of fresh water into your reservoir, you remain at the top of the escalator.

On the other hand, once we clearly distinguish which parts of life (material comforts and digital entertainment) are becoming ever cheaper and richer as the social pie expands, and which parts (scarce positional goods) require a zero-sum bidding war, we reclaim **the right to choose our arena**. You can walk away with complete equanimity from an auction you don't actually care about, savoring the absolute abundance of technological progress rather than drifting in nameless anxiety or hiding behind hollow platitudes about "opting out of the rat race."

*(Note: Of course, a centuries-old assumption inside the pyramid above is beginning to fracture—when AI turns expensive "specialized intellect" itself into an infinitely replicable, deflationary commodity like manufactured goods, how violently will the boundary between the third and fourth tiers be redrawn? That question is big enough to deserve an essay of its own.)*

## Re-examining Three "Conventional" Financial Moves Through PPM

Once we shift our lens from *nominal dollars* to *Parts Per Million of societal wealth (PPM)*, several "prudent" practices canonized by conventional financial textbooks reveal a very different complexion.

To keep our derivation rigorous, let's lock in our single governing equation:

> **Your PPM = Your Personal Net Worth ÷ Total U.S. Household Net Worth (currently ≈ $200 trillion)**

Over the past several decades, fueled by monetary expansion and compounding technological productivity, the denominator (total U.S. household net worth) has grown at a long-term annualized rate of **6.5%–7%**. With that moving target in mind, let's examine three everyday financial decisions:

### 1. Parking Cash in HYSA / T-Bills to "Live Off Interest": The Sneakiest Way to Short Human Progress

During high-interest-rate cycles, many people love parking large sums in high-yield savings accounts (HYSAs) or short-term Treasuries (like SGOV). Earning a risk-free 4.5%–5% while official CPI sits at 2.5% feels like a foolproof win.

Under the PPM microscope, however, it is guaranteed shrinkage:

1. **Tax Drag**: As long as you don't sell an equity index, unrealized capital gains compound tax-deferred. Interest income, by contrast, is taxed every single year at your top marginal ordinary-income rate (for high earners in high-tax states, often 40%+ combined federal and state). A 4.5% nominal yield frequently leaves just **2.5%–2.8%** after tax.
2. **Crushed by the Denominator**: While your numerator crawls forward at 2.6% a year, the $200 trillion denominator—propelled by equities and core real estate—is compounding ahead at roughly **7%**.

Put differently, **holding cash long-term to clip interest is effectively shorting the progress of human productivity.** So long as society continues generating excess returns through technological innovation, anyone living purely off interest watches their PPM share evaporate by **roughly 4% a year**. You think you are preserving principal; in reality, you are quietly surrendering your seat at the auction table.

### 2. DCA vs. Lump-Sum Coasting: The "Piton Effect" of Fresh Capital

Returning to our earlier metaphor: if buying SPY is like hammering pitons into a rock face, what is the real difference between buying once and coasting (*Lump Sum*) versus continuously investing (*Dollar-Cost Averaging*)?

The answer lies in **where the "new money" goes.**

Strictly speaking, if at 35 you buy SPY worth 10 PPM of national wealth (about $2 million) and never touch it or add a dime, does your societal share stay constant forever? Not quite—it suffers from a subtle **passive leakage**. Capital markets constantly metabolize: public companies issue stock-based compensation (SBC) that dilutes existing shareholders every year, while the most explosive emerging unicorns (like early OpenAI or SpaceX) often compound to hundreds of billions in private-market valuation before finally being added to public indices at a premium. Relying solely on old capital to roll forward means your numerator trails the broader wealth pool by half a beat.

**The essence of continuous investing is using a steady stream of external "new money" to forcibly offset that dilution—and actually grow your share.**

Whether that fresh capital comes from cashing out Silicon Valley equity grants or from the monthly savings of a salaried worker, the second it enters SPY, every dollar is mathematically equal: you are converting the *flow purchasing power* created by your current labor into *permanent equity ownership* of society's core productive assets.

- **No withdrawals, no new contributions**: You are playing defense with old chips, barely holding your current tier against dilution.
- **No withdrawals, continuously hammering new money in**: Not only do you lock in your original share, you forcibly expand your claim on society with every paycheck—10, 11, 12 PPM... For ordinary people, this is the only deterministic ladder up the purchasing-power pyramid.

### 3. Asset Diversification: Trading "Share Shrinkage" for Psychological Comfort

Traditional wealth advisors love preaching the classic "60/40 stock-bond portfolio" or cross-asset diversification in the name of dampening volatility. Viewed from the first principles of PPM, however, we have to ask: **when your core holding is already SPY—a basket of 500 of the most profitable companies on earth—what does it actually mean to "diversify" into low-yielding bonds, cash, or defensive ballast?**

It means you are **voluntarily diluting your own growth engine.**

Inside a $200 trillion wealth pool, the price of admission to top-tier scarce resources (T4 and T5 tickets) is always set by the fastest-compounding equities at the front of the pack. When you shift 40% of your portfolio into bonds yielding 3%–4% just to smooth out your account statement, you are voluntarily forfeiting that capital's ability to keep pace with societal wealth expansion.

Is diversification, then, completely useless? No—it serves exactly one real function: **providing liquidity insurance for people who have no external income.**

- **If you are still in your earning years (with steady new cash flow to invest)**: You have no need to hold bonds just to calm paper volatility. When the market plunges 30%, you aren't forced to sell shares to buy groceries; a market crash is simply society putting future equity ownership on sale for your incoming paycheck. Over-diversifying during your accumulation phase is self-sabotage.
- **Only when you are fully retired (having severed active income while locked into a rigid 4% annual withdrawal)**: Deprived of the shield of external cash flow, and terrified that a 50% drawdown will force you to liquidate shares at the bottom (*sequence-of-returns risk*), you have no choice but to hold bonds and cash.

The upshot: **conservative diversification is never a free lunch. It is a long-term *PPM shrinkage tax* that rentiers pay to insure against short-term ruin.**
